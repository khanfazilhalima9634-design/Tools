Down Payment Calculator

A Down Payment Calculator is a free online tool that helps homebuyers estimate the amount they need to pay upfront when purchasing a property. A down payment is one of the most important factors in home financing because it affects your mortgage amount, monthly payment, and total interest paid over the life of the loan. This calculator instantly estimates your required down payment, remaining loan amount, monthly mortgage payment, and total repayment based on the home price, down payment percentage, interest rate, and loan term.

Whether you are buying your first home, upgrading to a larger property, or investing in real estate, this calculator helps you understand how different down payment amounts impact your mortgage and overall borrowing costs.

What is a Down Payment Calculator?

A Down Payment Calculator is an online financial planning tool that calculates the upfront payment required when purchasing a home. It also estimates the remaining mortgage amount after subtracting the down payment from the purchase price, allowing buyers to better understand their financing needs.

How to Use the Down Payment Calculator

  1. Enter the home purchase price.
  2. Enter your desired down payment percentage.
  3. Enter the annual mortgage interest rate.
  4. Select the loan repayment term.
  5. Select the payment frequency.
  6. Click Calculate Down Payment.

The calculator estimates your upfront down payment, remaining mortgage loan amount, estimated payment, total interest and total repayment based on the information you enter.

The calculator will instantly display:

  • Required Down Payment
  • Mortgage Loan Amount
  • Estimated Monthly Mortgage Payment
  • Total Interest Payable
  • Total Amount Repaid

How to Calculate a Down Payment

A down payment is calculated by multiplying the home purchase price by the down payment percentage.

Down Payment Formula:
Down Payment = Home Purchase Price × Down Payment Percentage ÷ 100

After calculating the down payment, subtract it from the home purchase price to determine the amount that needs to be financed.

Mortgage Loan Amount Formula:
Loan Amount = Home Purchase Price − Down Payment

Example

If a home costs $400,000 and you choose a 20% down payment:

  • Home Price = $400,000
  • Down Payment Percentage = 20%
  • Down Payment = $400,000 × 20% = $80,000
  • Mortgage Loan Amount = $400,000 − $80,000 = $320,000

The calculator then uses the mortgage amount, interest rate and loan term to estimate the periodic payment and total interest.

Down Payment Calculator Example

Suppose you are purchasing a home for $400,000 and want to make a 20% down payment. You enter the following information into the calculator:

  • Home Price: $400,000

The down payment is calculated as:

$400,000 × 20% = $80,000

The remaining mortgage amount is:

$400,000 − $80,000 = $320,000

The calculator then uses the $320,000 mortgage amount, 6.25% interest rate, and 30-year loan term to estimate the monthly mortgage payment, total interest, and total repayment.

This example shows how changing the down payment percentage can change the amount you need to finance and your overall mortgage costs.

How Much Should You Put Down on a House?

There is no single down payment percentage that is appropriate for every homebuyer. The amount you choose can depend on the home price, mortgage program, lender requirements, interest rate, available savings and your overall financial situation.

A larger down payment generally means borrowing less money. If the interest rate and loan term remain the same, a smaller mortgage balance will generally result in a lower payment and less interest over the life of the loan.

However, putting more money into a home upfront can reduce the amount of cash available for emergency savings, closing costs, moving expenses and other homeownership costs. Compare different percentages with the calculator before deciding how much to put down.

Factors That Affect Your Down Payment

Home Purchase Price

Higher property prices generally require a larger down payment if the same percentage is used.

Down Payment Percentage

A higher down payment reduces the mortgage amount, lowers monthly payments, and decreases total interest paid.

Mortgage Interest Rate

Interest rates influence your monthly mortgage payment and the total cost of financing after the down payment is applied.

Loan Term

Longer repayment terms reduce monthly payments but increase the total interest paid throughout the mortgage.

Tips Before Making a Down Payment

  • Save as much as possible before purchasing a home.
  • Compare mortgage offers from multiple lenders.
  • Keep enough emergency savings after making your down payment.
  • Consider property taxes, insurance, and maintenance costs.
  • Choose a down payment amount that balances affordability and long-term savings.

Frequently Asked Questions About Down Payments

How do I calculate a down payment?

To calculate a down payment, multiply the purchase price by the down payment percentage. For example, if a home costs $400,000 and you make a 20% down payment, you would pay $80,000 upfront and finance the remaining $320,000.

How much down payment do I need for a house?

The amount of down payment you need depends on the home price, mortgage type, lender requirements, and your financial situation. You can use the Down Payment Calculator above to compare different down payment percentages and estimate how much you may need upfront.

How does a down payment calculator work?

A down payment calculator uses the purchase price and down payment percentage to estimate the amount you need to pay upfront and the remaining amount that needs to be financed.

How does a down payment affect my monthly payment?

A larger down payment reduces the amount you need to borrow. With the same interest rate and loan term, borrowing less generally results in a lower monthly payment.

Can I calculate my down payment from the home price and percentage?

Yes. Multiply the home price by the down payment percentage expressed as a decimal. For example, a 15% down payment on a $400,000 home is $60,000.

What is the difference between a down payment and the loan amount?

The down payment is the portion of the purchase price you pay upfront. The loan amount is the remaining amount that you finance. For example, if a home costs $300,000 and you pay $60,000 upfront, the remaining loan amount would be $240,000 before other costs or adjustments.

What percentage should I use for a house down payment?

There is no single down payment percentage that is right for everyone. The amount can vary depending on the mortgage programme, lender, property, location, and your financial circumstances. Compare several percentages with the calculator to see how they affect the amount financed.

Does a larger down payment reduce my monthly mortgage payment?

Generally, yes. A larger down payment means a smaller mortgage balance. If the interest rate and loan term stay the same, a smaller loan normally results in a lower monthly payment.
